What to do now

  1. Tell your flight surgeon about this result without waiting for the routine report.

    This tool’s own rule, not NASA’s

    Why

    The change in your right eye is at NASA’s clinically concerning level, where NASA decides case by case what to do. Telling the flight surgeon early in these cases is this tool’s own rule: NASA’s is not public.

  2. Repeat the scan of both eyes on flight day 139 (in 21 days).

    This tool’s own rule, not NASA’s

    Why

    At the clinically concerning level, this tool asks for the next scan 3 weeks later. NASA does not publish how often to repeat the scan at this level. This interval is this tool’s own rule, based on the one published case, where the scan was repeated every 3 to 6 weeks.

  3. Do the Amsler grid three times a week, one eye at a time.

    This tool’s own rule, not NASA’s

    Why

    Between scans, the grid can show a distortion at the centre of your vision. In the one published case it was used several times a week; this frequency is this tool’s own rule.

  4. For near work, try the stronger pair of your on-board glasses.

    NASA practice

    Why

    You reported worse near vision. Worse near vision is the most common change on long flights, and NASA flies at least three pairs of glasses of increasing strength per crew member for it. Glasses correct the blur, not its cause, and a change of prescription cannot be measured on board: this is something to try, not a measurement.

What this tool does not tell you

  • This is not a diagnosis: it is a first reading of a measurement against NASA’s published thresholds.
  • The exams in this demonstration are simulated; the analysis that measures them is real.
  • This tool has not been validated on people. Its ranges and probabilities come from a provisional error model that has not been measured yet.
  • Your flight surgeon on the ground confirms every result and has the final word.
  • The pressure inside the skull cannot be measured on board.
  • It does not measure the choroid, the layer of blood vessels under the retina.
  • It does not look at the macula, the centre of the retina: the scan covers only the area around the optic nerve.
  • It does not measure flattening of the back of the eye.
  • It does not measure a change in your glasses prescription: there is no reliable way to do that on board.
